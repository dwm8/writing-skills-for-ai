Apply to all person-facing writing. Replace habits, not banned words.
Show importance through concrete consequences/dependencies.

## Truth and mechanics

Support claims; never fabricate experience, precision, prevalence, foils or unfalsifiable findings.
No em dashes, en-dash substitutes, curly quotes/apostrophes or bold-label bullets.
Use contractions unless register excludes them; use consistent, meaningful headings.

## Contrast

State the positive claim; contrast only real, informative alternatives. Watch for
not-X-but-Y, trailing negatives, imperative substitutions, diagnosis/concession frames,
reversed pairs, opposed sentences, paired templates, antithesis (including semicolons),
negative-defined nouns and implied foils via actually/really/truly/genuinely/merely/in-practice/on-paper.

## Figuration

Keep informative figures; explain mechanisms behind unearned X-is-Y reframes.
Prefer plain verbs and one imagery domain. Avoid costume metaphors, figurative closers,
explaining images afterward, warmth-only idioms and coined labels without context.

## Clarity

Resolve referents; check claims against their named subjects. Develop before compressing;
define before shorthand. Omit points you cannot adequately develop. State each point once.

## Rhythm

Let content determine sentence/paragraph length. State certainty; hedge uncertainty plainly,
without stacking. Avoid repeated quotable closers, verdict callbacks, formulaic triads,
fragment chains/punchlines, throat-clearing, mechanical transitions and padding.
Natural triads, fragments and ordinary "that's why" are fine.

Truth/mechanics are strict. Judge other patterns by frequency/function; preserve natural
variation and useful devices instead of uniform compliance.
